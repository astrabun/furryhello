# furries: please don't just say "hello"

Imagine calling someone on the phone, going "hello!" and then putting them on hold. That's what a bare "hi" or "hello" DM does - except now the other person doesn't even know _why_ they're on hold, or for how long.

This happens a lot in the furry fandom. Two common patterns:

- **The check-in ghost.** Someone messages "hi" or "how are you" every couple of months, with nothing else. It feels like maintaining a friendship, but there's no actual conversation - just a occasional ping that goes nowhere.
- **The boredom ping.** Someone messages "hey" or "hello" daily (or more), not because they have something to say, but because they're bored. It's not a conversation starter, it's a reflex - and it puts the burden of generating substance on the other person.

Both patterns waste time and create a false sense of connection without real communication.

## ❌ Don't do this

{% chat %}
{% message "them" %}hi{% endmessage %}
{% message "you" %}hey! what's up?{% endmessage %}
{% message "them" %}nothing much, u?{% endmessage %}
{% message "you" %}same, just working{% endmessage %}
{% endchat %}

Nobody said anything. Two people just spent four messages confirming that neither of them had anything to say - and if this happens every few weeks with no other contact, it's not really a friendship being maintained, it's just noise.

## ✅ Instead try this

{% chat %}
{% message "them" %}hey! saw that art post post you shared, are you getting that YCH?{% endmessage %}
{% message "you" %}thinking about it! still deciding on which character I want to use for it tbh{% endmessage %}
{% message "them" %}oh nice, what are you leaning towards?{% endmessage %}
{% endchat %}

Same amount of effort to type, but now there's an actual topic. The other person can respond whenever they're free, and the conversation has somewhere to go.

## What to do instead

- Lead with the topic: "hey, did you end up going to that meetup?" beats "hi" every time.
- If you don't have a topic, that's fine - but don't send a message that's just a placeholder for one.
- If you're reaching out because you're bored, it's okay to just say that: "bored and thought of you, how's the suit coming along?" gives the other person something to respond to.
- Asking your real question up front also means the other person can answer async - they don't have to be sitting there staring at a lone "hello" wondering what you wanted.

This isn't about being blunt or rude - you can still be warm and friendly. It's about giving the other person something to actually respond to, instead of making them do the work of turning your "hello" into a conversation.
